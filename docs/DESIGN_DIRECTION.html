Design direction · v1 · 23 Aug 2026

A visa file you can watch move.

A direction for Trust Gate Overseas built on one idea: in a market crowded with agents who go quiet after taking a deposit, the product that shows its work wins. Navy and antique gold, as briefed — refined toward the vernacular of the document itself.

The brief, restated

Who this is for

Families and professionals in Punjab and the UK applying for Schengen, UK, Canada, Australia and US visitor visas. They are not shopping for the cheapest agent — they are trying to work out who is real. The site's one job is to convert that suspicion into a started application.

The design consequence

Every screen should answer "how do I know?" before it answers "how much?". Proof is the layout, not a testimonial slider bolted to the bottom.

Where the reference mockup is strong

The headline split ("The trip is yours. The paperwork is ours."), the live application-progress card in the hero, and the navy/gold restraint. All kept.

Where it's weak — and what changes

Status colour is undifferentiated, so "Approved" and "Action required" carry the same visual weight. Country cards read as a stock-photo grid with no information on them. Type is one neutral sans doing every job, so data and prose look identical. All three are fixed below.

01 · Colour

Six values, three jobs

Brand (navy, gold), surface (vellum, paper), and status (teal, ochre). Status colours are deliberately not brand colours — an approval should never be mistakable for a button.

Passport Navy
#0F1B2D
Ground. Hero, admin rail, footer. Warmer and deeper than your current #0a0e1c — reads as cover board, not as a dark-mode UI.
Seal Gold
#B98B2E
The only call-to-action colour on the site. Buttons pair it with navy text, never white — that's what keeps it premium rather than amber.
Gold Ink
#8A6518
Gold for text. Seal Gold fails contrast at small sizes on white; this is the accessible sibling for eyebrows and links.
Vellum
#F2EFE7
Section banding and case-file headers. Used in strips, never as the whole page background.
Stamp Teal

Verified · Approved · Submitted. The one addition to your palette, and the risk I'm taking — see the note below.
Action Ochre
#A8410F
Action required · Rejected. Never used decoratively, so its appearance always means something.

The one risk, and why I'd take it

Adding a third hue to a navy/gold system is normally a mistake. Here it's load-bearing: this product's entire promise is status legibility, and status needs a colour that isn't already spoken for by the brand. Teal is the ink of an approval stamp, so it's borrowed from the subject rather than invented. If you'd rather stay strictly two-colour, say so and I'll carry status on iconography and weight instead — it will be quieter and slightly harder to scan.

02 · Typography

Three faces, one each

Your repo currently loads Inter, Fraunces and JetBrains Mono. I'd keep Inter, swap Fraunces, and repurpose the mono into an actual role instead of an unused import.

DISPLAY · NEWSREADER 300–400headlines only · tracking −0.02em
The trip is yours.
The paperwork is ours.

Newsreader over Fraunces: Fraunces' soft, wobbling axis reads friendly-startup. Newsreader has optical sizing and a quieter, editorial cut — closer to a broadsheet travel page and to the officialdom of the documents themselves. Used at large sizes only, never below 24px.

BODY & UI · INTER 400–60015–17px · forms, prose, navigation
Visitor and tourist visa support for UK and India residents. A named case manager reviews every file, and you can see exactly where yours sits at any hour.

Kept. Inter is the right call for a product with this many forms and tables, and it's already tuned in your build.

DATA · IBM PLEX MONO 400–500refs, dates, statuses, eyebrows
#TG-2026-045  ·  SCHENGEN / FRANCE  ·  FILED 12 AUG 2026
DOCUMENTS VERIFIED  ·  DECISION AWAITING  ·  ACTION REQUIRED

This is the change that does the most work. Every reference number, date and status in the product renders in mono — so a glance tells you whether you're reading writing or reading record. IBM Plex Mono over JetBrains Mono for its civic, institutional register.

03 · Status system

Four states, everywhere, identical

The same four pills appear in the hero, the client portal and the admin tables. One vocabulary across the whole product means a client and a case manager are looking at the same word for the same thing.

Verified
Checked by a case manager. Nothing for the client to do.
Under review
With us. Waiting on our side, not theirs.
Action required
Blocked on the client. Always paired with what to do next.
Not started
Neutral by design — an empty step shouldn't feel like a failure.

05 · Build order

What I'd do, in sequence

Roughly the order I'd work in if you approve this direction. Each step leaves the app in a working state.

  1. Reconnect the tokensRewrite globals.css so --primary, --accent and friends resolve to the navy/gold system instead of the bright blue and amber currently overriding it. Add teal and ochre as status tokens. This alone re-skins every existing page.
  2. Type rolesSwap Fraunces for Newsreader, JetBrains Mono for IBM Plex Mono, and add a .font-data utility. Apply mono to every reference, date and status across all 40 pages.
  3. Status primitiveOne <StatusPill> component with the four states, replacing the ad-hoc badges. Used by portal, admin and public alike.
  4. Public site, 15 pagesHomepage first (hero + tracker + destinations), then destinations, service detail, eligibility flow, updates, blog, about, FAQ, contact, legal.
  5. Client portal, 7 pagesCase-file band, documents, messages, appointments, payments, profile.
  6. Admin panel, 15 pagesRail, KPI row, table system, then each management screen onto the shared table and dialog primitives.
  7. Quality floorMobile down to 360px, visible keyboard focus rings, prefers-reduced-motion respected, and contrast checked on every gold-on-light pairing.

Before you approve: tell me if the teal status colour stays or goes, and whether Newsreader is a yes — those two decisions set everything downstream.

Colours, type and layout in this document are design proposals, not verified brand assets. Copy shown is placeholder written to demonstrate tone; the reference numbers, names and dates are invented for illustration.
